# Respawn — authoritative product context

The name remains **Respawn**. The user explicitly confirmed it at handoff.

## Purpose and audience
A calm placement-preparation companion for Indian college students preparing for entry-level software developer roles. It should answer “what should I do today?” using knowledge, actual time, campus hiring expectations and target roles. It reduces confusion and helps students return after setbacks. No placement guarantees and no readiness score inferred from task completion.

## Latest decisions supersede earlier ones
AI assistance is now allowed. Fixed 2/3/4/5/6-month tracks are retired. Ask roughly when companies begin visiting campus, not an exact placement date. Unknown dates need an honest short rolling plan. Ask target companies/roles optionally; never invent company hiring dates, eligibility or question frequencies. DSA remains the central reviewed bank; add SQL, aptitude and core-CS activities deliberately. Revision is core, not an add-on. ₹700 advanced rescheduling is a future proposal, not an approved recurring subscription or per-event charge.

## Onboarding and motivation
First ask very little: target, approximate campus season, time available now. Give a small meaningful activity before requesting detailed availability and levels. Let students skip the warm-up or say they are unsure. Collect detail progressively. Separate studied-before, confident-now and solved-independently. Early plans should say provisional if important context is missing. Warmth and humour should create agency and trust, not guilt, addictive patterns, artificial scarcity or hidden paywalls.

## Scheduling
Use the entire supplied 196-question bank, with original IDs. Cover representative array/string/search/hash/window patterns before exhausting repetitive number/pattern exercises. Include needed prerequisites recursively. Longer available windows permit more variations and advanced work, but there is no fixed question count by month. Time estimates are assumptions to validate. The exact current bank lives in public/dsa-bank.js, not in a fabricated external sheet.

Task types include concepts, practice and revision. Each consumes actual capacity. Reserve catch-up room. Respect unavailable dates and whole-session requirements. Show included, deferred and completed as different states. Deferred questions can be struck through only with a clear Deferred label and reason. Pin/Defer/Restore controls generate a preview before replacing the active plan.

The existing pure planner is a heuristic, not a proven globally optimal solver. AI can rank approved tasks or explain choices; it must not bypass feasibility, prerequisite or history checks. The current AI bridge only suggests DSA priority IDs. Do not claim unrestricted AI plan generation already exists.

## Short days
A student with 10–20 minutes can be offered due recall/revision or a fitting SQL/aptitude/OS/DBMS/OOP/CN activity. These subjects are not inherently easy. Choose an appropriate small learning objective for the student's knowledge. A day off is valid. Do not record fictitious progress or hide displaced DSA. Repeated short days should lead to a realistic change in availability, scope or date assumptions.

## Revision and recovery
Use active recall, independent retry, mistakes and mixed practice. A 1/3/7/14-day pattern is an optional starting rule, not compulsory every time. Independent success can lengthen spacing; needed help should shorten it; failure can trigger a prerequisite refresher. Count unique solved questions separately from reviews. Do not count estimated time as measured hours. Preserve attempt history even when a task is deferred.

Replanning should preserve completed work; consider catch-up/free space first; show what moved, what was added and what was deferred; never increase daily hours or move deadlines without the student choosing it. Date changes do not justify regenerating the plan on every login. Use atomic cloud saves and stale-version rejection.

## Impossible timelines
Show the available hours, estimated workload and shortfall. Recommend revision to previously prepared students; beginners need genuine foundational learning, not “revision.” Be direct without telling someone their future is hopeless. Offer smaller scope, more hours or more calendar time when possible. If campus season has begun, say so; continuing beyond it must not imply those opportunities will wait. No payment can fix mathematical infeasibility.
