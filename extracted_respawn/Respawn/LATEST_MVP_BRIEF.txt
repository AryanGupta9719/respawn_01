PART A — UNDERSTAND THE PRODUCT FIRST
==================================================

## A1. What this is
[WEBSITE NAME] is a placement-prep companion for Indian college students (B.Tech/BCA/MCA, mostly Tier 2/3 colleges) preparing for entry-level software developer jobs.

It is NOT a content platform. It does NOT host problems, videos, or solutions.
Students already follow a sheet (Striver A2Z, our own 196-question DSA bank, etc.). We add the layer on top: daily plan, revision, honest timeline math, recovery after missed days, and small study groups.

## A2. The USP (every decision must serve this)
"Fell behind on placement prep? You're not starting over. [WEBSITE NAME] rebuilds a realistic plan from today, using the sheet you already follow."

Three pillars:
1. Comeback, not perfection — missing days is normal; the app makes restarting easy and guilt-free.
2. Honest math — show available hours vs required hours vs shortfall. Never fake reassurance.
3. Works with your sheet — problems link out to their original pages.

## A3. Who it's for
Most important user: the student who has stopped preparing for a few days/weeks and is close to giving up.
Also: beginners, students who studied and forgot, students juggling college, students who keep dropping off.

## A4. Product principles (non-negotiable)
- Never promise placement or imply finishing tasks guarantees success.
- Never shame. No punishing streak resets. No artificial urgency.
- Never hide backlog. Moved/deferred work stays visible. Nothing is marked complete unless the student marked it.
- Never invent problems, links, company question patterns, hiring dates, or eligibility rules.
- Never show a "placement readiness %" or claim to measure mastery. Completion is self-reported and labelled so.
- Recovery/rescheduling is FREE. No paywalls anywhere in this MVP. No payment code.
- Mobile first. Most users are on phones.

## C3. Planning engine (/src/engine — deterministic, pure functions, unit tested)
Inputs: sheet items, student level, daily minutes per weekday, unavailable dates, hiring date/estimate, completed items + attempt history, current date.

Rules:
1. Capacity = sum of available minutes from today to hiring date (or 14 days if unknown).
2. Order items by sheet order, respecting prerequisites.
3. Level adjustments:
   - "Studied but forgot"/"Mostly ready": first pass on already-familiar basics uses shorter revision-style tasks (60% of estMinutes), labelled "Refresh", not "New".
   - "Starting from scratch": never label first-time learning as revision.
4. Short timeline (capacity < required): keep representative problems per topic (first easy + first medium per subtopic), defer extra variations. Never spend the whole plan on basics before reaching arrays, strings and hashing.
5. Revision: after an item is completed, schedule revisions at +1, +3, +7, +14 days (constant, configurable). Revision costs ~40% of estMinutes (min 10). Revision consumes real capacity — never stack it on a full day.
6. Daily fill: due revision first (if fits), then next new item(s) up to the day's minutes. Leave ~10% buffer as catch-up space.
7. Output: a dated schedule + a list of deferred items + summary numbers (available, required, shortfall).
8. Never mark anything complete. Never delete completed history. Never create duplicate tasks.
9. Validation function that checks every generated plan: no day over capacity, prerequisites respected, no duplicates, completed items preserved, dates valid. If validation fails, throw in dev and fall back to a simpler sequential plan in prod.

Write unit tests for: shortfall case, unknown hiring date, missed-5-days recovery, revision spacing, prerequisite order, light-day choice.

Save the accepted plan to the database. Do NOT regenerate on every login. Regenerate only on a trigger (see C6).

## C4. Today screen (home after login)
Shows only:
- Greeting + Kapi.
- ONE main task card: title, topic, est. time, "Open problem ↗" (external link), "Start (2 min)" button.
- Due revision card(s), if any.
- Day type selector: Normal day / Short session / Revision only / Take today off.
- Pod strip: who in your pod studied today (C7).

Task completion flow: tap "Done" → quick feedback: "Solved on my own" / "Needed help" / "Couldn't solve".
- Solved on my own → normal revision schedule.
- Needed help → next revision moved earlier (+1, +2, +5…).
- Couldn't solve → mark "attempted", schedule retry in 2 days, and if it has a prerequisite not completed, suggest it.
- Optional field: paste submission link (stored, labelled "proof added").
Copy example: "One down. Smooth."

Short session: ask "How many minutes?" (10/20/30/custom) → pick due revision first, else the smallest suitable pending item. If the main task moves, show "Moved to Thu, 30 Oct".
If the student chooses short sessions 4+ times in 7 days, show a gentle note: "Lots of short days lately. Want to block one longer session this week?" with a date picker. Never auto-change their hours.

Take today off: no guilt copy ("Rest is part of the plan."). Tasks shift; show what moved.

## C5. Plan screen
- Week view (default) + list of upcoming days.
- Deferred items list (always visible, with "Bring back" button).
- Summary bar: done / revisions done / topics covered / deferred. Show "unique problems solved" separately from "revision attempts".

## C6. Comeback / "Restart from today" (most important flow — polish this)
Triggers: 2+ days with no activity on scheduled days, availability change, hiring date change, sheet change, assessment date added.

When the student returns after missing 2+ scheduled days:
1. Screen 1 — no guilt: Kapi + "Life happened. Let's adjust." (or plain version with Less banter: "You missed a few days. Let's update your plan.")
2. Screen 2 — tiny win first: one small task that fits 10–15 minutes (prefer due revision). "Start" or "Skip to plan".
3. Screen 3 — replan preview: what moved, what was deferred, what stayed; new available vs required hours; honest note if the shortfall grew.
4. Buttons: "Save changes" / "Adjust plan" (edit availability first).
Rules: never dump all missed tasks onto tomorrow; protect prerequisites and due revision; use buffer days; preserve all history.
